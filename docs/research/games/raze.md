## 1. Who and what

- Raze (Kongregate listing): "Created by Justin Goncalves and Addison Rodomista" (handles Juice-Tin and AddisonR). Released 8 Mar 2010 on Kongregate (Flash Gaming Wiki snippet says 25 Feb 2010: conflicting). Description: "Battle against Aliens, Robots, and Zombies in this futuristic fight for the survival of earth. Or be the enemies and fight for the destruction of it." Dev note: "Raze won best shooter at the 2011 Flash Gaming Summit" (the note is dated 2010 on the page, so the date is odd) and promised a sequel. [read: Kongregate Raze page]
- Raze 2 (2011): "half-update/half-sequel". Raze 3 (Sky9 Games, circa Dec 2014 per a CrazyGames snippet) with Sky9 label. Strike Force Heroes 1 to 3 followed; JayIsGames: "Juice-Tin, part of the Sky9 team, was co-creator of the Raze series. ... a fair description would be 'Raze... NOT IN SPACE'." [read: JayIsGames reviews; snippet: MobyGames lists Sky9 Games 13 games 2009-2023]
- The 2023 Steam remake "Strike Force Heroes" (Sky9 Games, publisher IndieArk, 10 Nov 2023) says "The original 2-man development team behind the hit Flash series Strike Force Heroes and Raze are back." It is rebuilt with online co-op and PvP, 4 classes, "hundreds of weapons, abilities, and equipment", loot system, voiced campaign, daily missions. [read: Steam appdetails]

## 2. Core loop and fun (critics and players; no developer statements found)

- JayIsGames on Raze 2 (read): "fast, frenetic and fun... should your CPU be able to handle the load"; "streamlined gameplay, a host of new weapons, maps and play-modes, and one of the best soundtracks"; two-perspective campaign (human then alien: "you'll have a chance to take revenge on those slimy humans"); juvenile humour (a shot to the butt announces the kill); complaints: teammates hard to tell from enemies, no online multiplayer, high CPU use.
- On Raze 3 (read): the series "has brought the running and gunning action into the browser"; 15 campaign levels; rocket-repelling laser sword; "exhaustive upgrades system which is almost daunting"; every weapon tweakable "to the minutest detail"; "some of the new backgrounds and levels are fantastic"; "gameplay hasn't evolved much since previous entries but what's fun is fun". A reader says it was "Extremely Glitchy" on first release and lagged heavily.
- Kongregate Raze 1 comments (read): many players asked for multiplayer and Kongregate achievements; "Alien 23 and 24 should be 3v3. 26 should be 4v3" (players think the alien campaign's later missions are lopsided); "last level of the human campaign is easier than level 26 on alien campaign"; "Why was I programmed to feel pain?" (5/5). Users still play and reminisce in 2025-2026, so retention of nostalgia is strong.
- Core loop (derived): pick loadout, play short arena match (deathmatch/CTF/etc. against bots, in a side-scrolling platform arena with rapid respawn), earn credits, buy upgrades, repeat. Strike Force Heroes adds classes, kill-streak abilities and a campaign with a conspiracy plot.

## 3. Movement [numbers unavailable]

Controls (read: JIG): WASD or arrows move; mouse aims and shoots; Q/E cycle weapons; number keys select; F uses abilities in Raze 2 (in Raze 3, F or Enter uses the laser sword). Raze 3 also has an Agility equipment category (screenshot). [snippet: Poki lists scroll wheel to switch weapons]. Crouch exists. A grenade key (Q in one aggregator) is **unverified**. SFH: WASD/arrows, mouse, shift/Q to swap, ctrl or E for kill-streak ability, SFH2 lets right-click be assigned to reload, ability or swap. [read: JIG]. Run speed, jump height, double jump, jetpack, wall interaction, ladders: **not published**. [memory: the characters jump with an arcing platformer jump and fall quickly; unverified]

## 4. Combat and weapons

Raze 1: "12 sets of characters" to customize, "six different battle locations", five quick-match modes: Deathmatch (1-9 opponents), Team Deathmatch (2-5 per team), Elimination (limited respawns 7-25), Team Elimination (15-25, friendly-fire toggle), and a juggernaut-style mode (text cut off). Settings: opponents, difficulty, score to win, "Damage Mode", "Ego Mode", "Ballistics". Campaign: human and alien, 15 levels each; alien unlocked after human; early levels are tutorial bots. [read: ilovefreesoftware]

Raze 2 weapon picture (mostly [snippet]): about 35 weapons from modern to futuristic, in seven categories each with a distinctive special ability; starter, sidearm, close range, automatic, long range, heavy etc.; Holy Grail (one-shot long-range, "1 shot, 1 kill"); Flamethrower (heavy); Plasma pistol (alien weapon, 850 credits, homing shots); Railgun recommended for long range; Electro Bolt expensive; equipment list: Katana, Grenades, Mines, Teleport, Ammo Refill, Cloaking, Heal Kit, Static Field; Cloak or Transport called the best ability; credits unlock abilities, weapons, skins and equipment. [snippet: Raze 2 wiki, Armor Games threads, aggregator]. Perks mentioned by commenters: ammo regeneration, 70% ability recharge. Killstreaks of 30+ with sniper rifles were the only way past some levels, and the Raze soldier boss in Raze 1/2 has about 3x normal health, carries a rocket launcher that kills in two hits, and must be killed six times in four minutes. [read: JIG Raze 2 comments]

Raze 3 loadout screen (read: image from JIG): slots Side Arm (M9 Barret), Automatic (Assault Rifle), Close Range (Shotgun), Long Range (.50 Cal), Explosive (Rocket Launcher), then Agility and three equipment slots; armour sets such as "ERT Mk I Combat Ark - Works in Human Campaign".

Strike Force Heroes classes: SFH1 Medic, Commando, Assassin, Tank (level up each class by kills; unlock class-specific weapons); SFH2 Engineer, Mercenary, General, Juggernaut, Sniper; SFH3 eight classes (Engineer, Juggernaut, Mercenary, Sniper, Gunslinger, Medic, Elite, Ninja, later Knight) with elemental/experimental energy weapons and status effects, randomly generated heroes, slot machine rewards. [read: JIG; snippet: TV Tropes, SFH wiki]. Win conditions: kill counts, holding points, capture the flag, killing a single juggernaut boss. Ammo and health packs are scattered around levels. [read: JIG]

Numbers (damage, fire rate, magazine, projectile speed, enemy types in the campaign beyond human/alien/robot/zombie): **unavailable**.

## 5. Destruction

Not a feature. Raze 3 listed a "physics" category on JayIsGames and SFH2's Japanese mansion "quickly gets totally wrecked with bullet-shells and bodies" (visual debris only). Rockets and explosives deal area damage; there is no tile destruction. [read: JIG]

## 6. Game feel and presentation

JIG: SFH art is "sleek, even a little cartoony"; soundtrack of riffs on "When Johnny Comes Marching Home"; Raze 2 soundtrack praised. Raze 1: "breathtakingly visuals" for Flash but laggy; devs advise lowering graphics and using popup window. Respawn is instant and unlimited in deathmatch modes. Kill-streak abilities reward unbroken streaks; the UI draws a red X on enemy kills (remake screenshots) and floating damage numbers. No published info on camera shake or hit-pause. [read]

## 7. Level design, modes, progression

- Raze 1: Quick Match (5 modes) and Campaign (human then alien) with unlock chain; per-round credits for upgrades. Weapons unlock with credits and categories. [read]
- Raze 3: 15 campaign levels, deep per-weapon upgrades, avatar customization. [read]
- SFH: arena levels with distinct themes (jungle village, disintegrating aircraft, secret underwater lab, space station, third-world marketplace, Japanese mansion); win conditions vary by level; campaign difficulty "takes quite a leap" later; classes feel unbalanced. [read: JIG]
- Co-op: Raze 1/2 AI teammates (campaign "with or without a partner by your side"); online co-op/PvP only in the 2023 remake. [read]

## 9. Reception

JIG user ratings: Raze 3 4.5/5 (39 votes); Poki 4.5 (about 17k votes); CrazyGames 9.3 (about 34.8k votes) [snippet]; Kongregate Raze 4.3. Praise: fast frenetic action, streamlined controls, upgrades, soundtrack, fun maps; criticism: lag/CPU use, no online multiplayer, balance (classes, late levels, boss health), weak story, generic space setting, hard-to-tell teammates, glitchy launches.

## 10. What our game should take from it (adapted, original)

1. **Slot-category loadouts.** Use clear weapon slots (sidearm, automatic, close range, long range, explosive) plus 3 gadget slots and an agility/utility slot, as in Raze 3's loadout screen.
2. **Deep per-weapon attachments.** Tweak grip, optic, barrel and stock with small effects; JIG says cosmetic-feeling but satisfying. Show tradeoffs plainly.
3. **Credits-after-round loop.** Every mission pays out credits for purchases, including partial credit on failure.
4. **Kill-streak abilities as optional boosts.** In loud play, a streak unlocks a short special (drone strike, overdrive); stealth play earns a different "ghost" meter instead.
5. **Distinct special-equipment list.** Cloak, teleport, heal kit, mines, static field map directly to our lockpick, drone, decoy, smoke, C4.
6. **Classes with distinct weapon pools.** Medic/Engineer/Sniper/Juggernaut archetypes as operative "kits" with one signature gadget each.
7. **Win-condition variety per mission.** Eliminate N, hold a point, capture an item, kill a boss: our infiltration can use steal/extract, plant explosive, hold the door.
8. **Bot-based arena practice.** Short arena scenarios against AI at configurable difficulty ("Ego Mode", "Damage Mode", "Ballistics" toggles) as a training range for new weapons.
9. **Name tags and team-coloured health bars.** But make allies unmistakable, since JIG flags teammates vs enemies as a Raze 2 flaw; use shape and colour.
10. **One-shot special weapons.** A high-skill single-shot weapon (like Holy Grail) that dominates unless countered by cover; give guards counter-play.
11. **Boss as a recurring named mini-boss with 3x HP.** Cautionary note: 3x health plus a two-hit rocket was a frustration wall; instead telegraph and make the boss weak to environmental traps.
12. **Performance guard-rails.** CPU load is the most-cited complaint across all titles; give a graphics preset menu and a "reduce particles" toggle on day one (three.js: cap particles, instancing).
13. **Optional online/AI co-op.** Players asked for multiplayer in every comment thread; plan data structures that can host an AI partner now and a real one later.
14. **Per-class progression with XP, cosmetic outfits.** Level classes via use, unlock class-specific weapons (SFH).
15. **Slot-machine / random reward screen** (SFH2/3) but keep it cosmetic/optional to avoid balance bugs.
16. **Randomized heroes plus perks** (SFH3) as a roguelite mode: random operative with fixed perks per run.
17. **Level themes and per-level modifiers.** Environment status effects (rain, darkness, low gravity) as JIG notes in SFH3.
18. **Instant respawn in training; strict in missions.** Match our modes to Raze's respawn knobs (infinite vs limited lives 7-25).
19. **Soundtrack identity.** Marching/military riff remixes in SFH gave identity; commission a short loopable theme per mission type.
20. **Fix glitches at launch.** Commenters remember Raze 3 as glitchy; ship fewer systems but stable ones.
